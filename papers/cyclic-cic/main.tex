\begin{abstract}
This note motivates a cyclic variant of a Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC)-like calculus in which proofs are finite graphs with cycles, validated by a global progress condition, rather than trees generated by a fixed family of induction principles.
This representation supports normalization-style transformations (commuting conversions, unfolding/refolding, etc.) that can \emph{identify} proofs that are distinct syntactically, yielding a form of post-hoc proof identity.
We relate this to normalization-by-evaluation: cyclic proofs can be normalized by evaluating them through their induction principles and reifying the result back into a canonical cyclic object, so dependent indices reduce definitionally and one avoids explicit equality rewrites/transport.
We outline the source term calculus, a fix-free cyclic proof/term language with explicit substitution evidence, the global condition, and representative transformations such as \textsc{CaseCase} and head beta-reduction.
\end{abstract}
