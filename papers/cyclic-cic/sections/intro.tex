\section{Introduction}

In conventional systems, recursive reasoning is packaged as a fixed-point operator or as a family of induction/recursion schemata.
Those choices are powerful, but they also hard-code a particular presentation of recursion into the syntax of proofs.
As a result, proofs that are semantically equivalent can remain syntactically far apart, even after many semantics-preserving transformations.

Cyclic proof theory suggests a different decomposition:
\begin{quote}
A proof is a finite graph of locally-correct steps; recursion is represented by cycles; and soundness is ensured by a global progress condition.
\end{quote}

This decomposition has an attractive consequence for proof engineering and normalization:
we can refactor recursive structure (fold/refold), commute conversions, and perform administrative normalization without being forced to preserve the exact boundaries of \texttt{fix} binders or induction schemata.
In the best case, normalization can reveal that two proofs are not just equivalent, but \emph{the same cyclic object} after refolding.

\paragraph{Scope and claims.}
This paper specifies a cyclic CIC-like calculus, its extracted-term semantics, and a normalization pipeline based on supercompilation.

The canonicalisation claim is deliberately \emph{operational}.
We can guarantee that the partial-evaluation component is complete for the chosen head-reduction strategy (i.e. driving to weak-head normal form does not miss head redexes).
For the full supercompilation bundle (driving + information propagation + commuting conversions + unfold/generalise/fold), a natural and desirable property is \emph{idempotence} (a second pass yields no further change); at present we treat this as a conjecture/engineering target rather than a proved meta-theorem.
However, we do \emph{not} claim that all semantically identical programs/proofs can always be reduced to a single unique normal form.
Rather, the goal is that many bureaucratic differences---especially those arising from commuting conversions, index computations, and recursion-boundary choices---are eliminated by the procedure.
