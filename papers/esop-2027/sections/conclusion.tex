\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that supercompilation for the Calculus of Constructions with
inductives is focused cyclic sequent proof search.  Driving, splitting,
folding, and generalisation correspond precisely to invertible sequent
rules, synchronous choice rules, cyclic backlinks, and cut introduction
respectively.  The operational distinction between invertible and
non-invertible steps instantiates focusing without requiring explicit
polarity annotations, and the budget-trace progress condition provides a
constructive termination guarantee.

The key consequences are threefold.  First, \textbf{induction principles are
discovered post-hoc}: the cycle structure of the proof graph \emph{is} the
induction principle, read off from the completed graph rather than
prescribed in advance.  This is especially valuable in dependent types,
where index computations must be normalised before rewriting and the
induction scheme must simultaneously simplify the index and prove the
property.  Second, \textbf{backlinks are admissible}: every valid cyclic
proof can be residualised to a standard CIC term using only the native
fixpoint binder, as shown in a companion paper~\cite{admissibility-2027}.
Third, the \textbf{correspondence is directional} but sufficient: every
successful supercompilation run yields a valid cyclic proof, and the
converse---whether every cyclic proof can be obtained by supercompilation---is
an open question related to the completeness of the driving/whistle
strategy.

The mechanised development relies only on functional extensionality, a
standard axiom of CIC that does not affect computational content.  We
leave the extension of the substitution lemma to fully parametric
inductive types (lifting the \texttt{closed\_param\_tys} condition by
threading parameter instantiation through the $\mathsf{roll}$ rule) as
future work, following the approach of MetaCoq~\cite{sozeau-etal-2023-metacoq-jar}.
