\paragraph{Open problem: backlink admissibility.}
Proving that the \textsc{back} rule is admissible---i.e., that every cyclic proof in our calculus corresponds to a standard inductive proof in CIC---remains an open problem. The difficulty lies in the interaction between cyclic backlinks and dependent elimination motives: each recursive call in the cyclic proof must correspond to a use of the CIC recursor whose motive varies with the type indices. Brotherston and Simpson established this correspondence for first-order logic~\cite{brotherston-simpson-2011-sequent}; extending it to CoC with inductives would be a significant new result. Our work sidesteps this via the CIU theorem: the residual is guaranteed observationally equivalent to the source, which suffices for program transformation correctness without requiring admissibility of the backlink rule.
