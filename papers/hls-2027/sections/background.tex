\section{Supercompilation as Cyclic Proof Search}
\label{sec:background}

The correspondence between supercompilation and cyclic proof search is the
foundation on which this paper builds.  We state it here directly; a
companion mechanisation formalises it in Rocq using only functional extensionality.

\emph{Notation.}  $\mathcal{S}(f,\Sigma,\Gamma,t,A)$ denotes the supercompiler
with fuel~$f$; $\mathcal{S}_{\!\mathit{tc}}$ adds the trace-condition check;
$\mathcal{R}(f,b,v)$ denotes residualisation from vertex~$v$ of the
configuration graph~$b$ with fuel~$f$; and $t\approx u$ abbreviates CIU
equivalence.\smallskip

\subsection{The CIU guarantee}

\begin{theorem}[CIU soundness, \texttt{supercompile\_ciu\_soundness\_untyped}]
\label{thm:ciu}
If the supercompiler with trace check succeeds on a well-typed goal,
$\mathcal{S}_{\!\mathit{tc}}(f,\Sigma,\Gamma,t,A) = \mathsf{Some}(v,b)$,
then the residual term read back from $b$ is observationally indistinguishable
from the source:
\[
  t \approx \mathcal{R}(f_r,\Sigma,b,v)
\]
where $t \approx u$ means $t$ and $u$ are CIU-equivalent
($\forall\sigma,w.\; t[\sigma]\Downarrow w \iff u[\sigma]\Downarrow w$).
\end{theorem}

The hypothesis mentions only the trace-check flag --- not how generalisation
was performed.  This is the key property exploited by the oracle architecture:
\emph{any} generalisation strategy is admissible, provided the resulting graph
passes \texttt{trace\_condition\_ok}.  The LLM is just one such strategy.
