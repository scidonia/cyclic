\section{What Remains}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{The $\omega$-rule pipeline}

The $\omega$-rule pipeline (LLM lemma proposal $\to$ sub-SC validation $\to$
lemma-driven SC) is demonstrated (Section~\ref{sec:reverse-append}): the LLM
proposes the lemma, the sub-SC validates it (\vmcompute{}), and the
lemma-driven SC uses it as a rewrite rule.

\subsection{Conditional lemmas}

Some auxiliary lemmas require a hypothesis.  The canonical example is
$\mathsf{evenp}\ n = \mathsf{false}$ under the hypothesis
$\mathsf{oddp}\ n = \mathsf{true}$, needed to prove
$\mathsf{any}\ \mathsf{evenp}\ (\mathsf{filter}\ \mathsf{oddp}\ l)
= \mathsf{false}$.  The current lemma environment supports only
unconditional rewrite rules.

The infrastructure for conditional lemmas is specified
(\texttt{ConditionalLemmas.v}): a lemma record with hypothesis fields, a
guarded rewrite check, and a \texttt{known\_hyps} mechanism that tracks
which hypotheses have been established by preceding vertices in the SC
graph.  The CIU soundness extension follows the unconditional proof
exactly, adding one premise: the hypothesis vertex must be an ancestor
of the vertex using the conditional lemma.  This is stated as the
\texttt{supercompile\_ciu\_soundness\_conditional} theorem; its
mechanised proof is structurally identical to the existing 1800-line
unconditional proof and requires approximately 100 additional lines.

\subsection{Commutativity}

$\mathsf{plus}\ m\ n = \mathsf{plus}\ n\ m$ remains unproved.  Both sides
SC to different-shaped residuals.  This requires a lemma environment
with the inductive equations for $\mathsf{plus}$, which the SC can prove
individually but cannot chain together without the lemma-driven loop.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The LLM lemma proposal succeeds on all 6 tested programs
  (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), including with scrambled names, and the
  proposed lemmas are validated by the sub-SC.  A larger corpus
  would strengthen the evidence.

\item The companion mechanisation (under review) provides the CIU theorem
  (\texttt{supercompile\_ciu\_soundness\_untyped}) on which the oracle
  architecture depends.  This paper states the proof-theoretic correspondence
  directly.

\item 33 of the 71 primary theorems are provable by standard SC alone
  (anti-unification) --- they calibrate the system.  16 theorems require
  the lemma-driven SC ($\omega$-rule), and 4 require dependent-type
  pruning.
\end{itemize}
