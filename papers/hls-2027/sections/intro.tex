\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Supercompilation~\cite{turchin-1986-supercompiler} is a powerful program
transformation technique that fuses composed functions, eliminates intermediate
data structures, and discovers recursive structure automatically.  Despite
decades of development, its proof-theoretic status has remained informal: the
community understands that folding ``corresponds to induction'' and that
generalisation ``corresponds to lemma introduction'', but these correspondences
have not been made precise enough to guide design.

Our starting point is an earlier mechanised result: standard supercompilation
is focused cyclic sequent proof search~\cite{TODO-prev-paper}.  Making that
correspondence precise in Rocq had an unexpected benefit --- it revealed, with
mathematical precision, \emph{two places where the standard theory is
incomplete}.

\paragraph{Gap 1: Generalisation = cut introduction.}
In the sequent calculus, driving is cut elimination (reduce, reduce, reduce)
and generalisation is cut introduction (invent a lemma whose proof is delegated
to a subgoal).  Cut introduction is the creative step in any proof.  Standard
SC uses syntactic anti-unification as its cut-introduction heuristic --- but
anti-unification is purely structural and cannot discover semantic identities
such as map fusion ($\map\ f \circ \map\ g = \map\ (f \circ g)$) or accumulator
patterns.  This is the domain of \emph{higher-level supercompilation}
(HLS)~\cite{sorensen-gluck-jones-1996-positive}.

\paragraph{Gap 3: Strengthened induction = conditional CIU.}
For correctness properties like $\mathsf{sorted}\ (\mathsf{sort}\ l) = \mathsf{true}$,
cyclic induction alone cannot close the proof: after one driving step the goal
becomes $\mathsf{sorted}\ (\ins\ x\ (\mathsf{sort}\ l)) = \mathsf{true}$, and the
fold back to $\mathsf{sorted}\ (\mathsf{sort}\ l)$ is blocked by the $\ins/\mathsf{sort}$
structure mismatch.  In proof theory this requires a \emph{strengthened induction
hypothesis} (the $\omega$-rule).  We show that the LLM can propose the necessary
auxiliary lemma, the sub-SC can prove it, and the main SC then uses it as a
rewrite rule --- closing the proof automatically.

\paragraph{Gap 2: Speculation = variable dependency analysis.}
Eisner and Blatz~\cite{eisner-blatz-2007-parsing} identify a transformation
they call \emph{speculation}: if a subcomputation is independent of some loop
variable, hoist it out of the loop.  This is loop-invariant code motion for
logic programs.  Making the sequent correspondence precise reveals why standard
SC cannot do this: anti-unification preserves all free variables by
construction, so it can never propose a generalised term with \emph{fewer}
free variables than the original.  The required operation is not
anti-unification but \emph{projection} --- dropping variables whose absence
from a subterm can be verified.

\paragraph{What we build.}
We implement all three extensions in a mechanised pipeline:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{LLM oracle for cut introduction} (Section~\ref{sec:llm}).
  A DeepSeek V4 Pro oracle proposes generalisations; validated by the
  kernel.  The oracle is untrusted for soundness.

\item \textbf{Speculation via dependency analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:speculation}).
  Verified dependency analysis, independent-subterm extraction, and
  projection generalisation, with a mechanised roundtrip theorem.

\item \textbf{LLM lemma proposal for the $\omega$-rule} (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
  When the SC is stuck and neither AU nor speculation unblocks it, the
  LLM proposes an auxiliary lemma.  A sub-SC run proves the lemma.
  The main SC retries with the lemma as a rewrite rule --- closing proofs
  that were previously out of reach.
\end{enumerate}

The generalisation strategy becomes a four-layer fallback:
\[
  \underbrace{\text{anti-unification}}_{\text{standard SC}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{speculation}}_{\text{new, mechanised}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{LLM generalisation}}_{\text{kernel-validated}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{LLM lemma}}_{\text{$\omega$-rule cut introduction}}
\]

\paragraph{Empirical results.}
The combined system proves the following entirely by \vmcompute{} (symbolic
execution to normal form), with no human-provided induction tactics:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Result} & \textbf{Category} & \textbf{Arch.} \\
\midrule
$\length\ (\map\ f\ l) = \length\ l$ & map-length fusion & AU \\
$\map\ f\ (\map\ g\ l) = \map\ (f \circ g)\ l$ & functor composition & AU \\
$\length\ (\mathsf{sort}\ l) = \length\ l$ & sort preserves length & AU \\
$\mathsf{sum}\ (\mathsf{sort}\ l) = \mathsf{sum}\ l$ & sort preserves sum & AU \\
\texttt{bind (return x) f = f x} & List monad left-id & AU \\
\texttt{bind l return = l} & List monad right-id & AU \\
\texttt{bind (bind l f) g = bind l ($\lambda$x.\ldots)} & List monad assoc & AU \\
\texttt{bind (return x) f = f x} & Maybe monad left-id & AU \\
\addlinespace
\texttt{sorted (sort l) = true} & insertion sort correctness & $\omega$-rule \\
\texttt{reverse (append xs ys) = append (reverse ys) (reverse xs)} & reverse-append distrib & $\omega$-rule \\
\texttt{sum (map succ xs) = sum xs + length xs} & succ distribution & $\omega$-rule \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
AU = proved by anti-unification alone (standard SC).
$\omega$-rule = requires LLM-proposed lemma, sub-SC validation, and
lemma-driven SC.

\paragraph{The methodological point.}
Our central claim is not merely that these results hold, but \emph{why} a
mechanised sequent-calculus foundation made them reachable.  The sequent
calculus identified the gaps; the mechanisation forced them to be stated
precisely; the precision made the implementations straightforward.
