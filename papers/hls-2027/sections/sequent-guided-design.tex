\section{How the Sequent Calculus Guided Design}
\label{sec:design}

The two extensions did not arise from intuition about supercompilation.  They
arose from asking a precise proof-theoretic question: \emph{what is
generalisation, exactly?}

\subsection{Generalisation is cut introduction}

In the sequent calculus, cut elimination is mechanical (reduce until no more
cuts remain).  Cut introduction is creative: it means inventing a formula $C$
such that the proof splits into ``prove $C$'' and ``use $C$ to prove the goal.''
Generalisation in SC is exactly this: invent a configuration $S_{\mathsf{gen}}$
such that both the current and companion configurations are instances of it.

Standard anti-unification is one algorithm for cut introduction, but it is
\emph{purely syntactic}: it finds the most specific common generalisation of
two terms as terms.  It cannot discover:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Fusion identities}: $\map\ f\ (\map\ g\ l)$ and $\map\ (f \circ g)\ l$
  are syntactically unrelated, but semantically equal.  Anti-unification
  produces $\map\ ?_0\ ?_1$ --- a trivial generalisation that enables no folding.
\item \textbf{Accumulator patterns}: $\mathsf{sum}\ (\mathsf{rev}\ l)$ does not
  share syntactic structure with $\mathsf{sum\_acc}\ l\ 0$, but the latter is
  the accumulator form that enables a tail-recursive residual.
\end{itemize}

Once we identified generalisation as cut introduction, the solution became
clear: replace the syntactic heuristic with an \emph{oracle} that can propose
cuts with semantic content.  The oracle need not be trusted --- the kernel
check (trace condition) is the soundness backstop.

\subsection{Speculation is variable projection}

The second gap emerged from a different question: \emph{why does anti-unification
always preserve all free variables?}

Anti-unification of $j_1$ and $j_2$ produces a $j_{\mathsf{gen}}$ such that
$j_1 = \sigma_1(j_{\mathsf{gen}})$ and $j_2 = \sigma_2(j_{\mathsf{gen}})$.
The free variables of $j_{\mathsf{gen}}$ are a \emph{subset} of those of $j_1$
and $j_2$ (holes abstract over differing subterms, but shared subterms retain
their variables).

Speculation requires something different: $j_{\mathsf{gen}}$ should have
\emph{strictly fewer} free variables than $j_1$ and $j_2$, having \emph{dropped}
variables that do not appear in the hoisted subterm.

\begin{example}[Eisner-Blatz]
The rule $\mathsf{constit}(X, I, K) \mathbin{:-} \mathsf{rewrite}(X, Y),\,
\mathsf{constit}(Y, I, K)$ has a subcomputation $\mathsf{rewrite}(X, Y)$
that does not mention $I$ or $K$.  Speculation hoists it:
\[
  \mathsf{temp}(X, Y) \mathbin{:-} \mathsf{rewrite}(X, Y)
  \qquad
  \mathsf{constit}(X, I, K) \mathbin{:-} \mathsf{temp}(X, ?_0),\, \mathsf{constit}(?_0, I, K)
\]
The key: $\mathsf{temp}$ has free variables $\{X, Y\}$, not $\{X, Y, I, K\}$.
\end{example}

This is impossible for anti-unification because: given
$j_1 = \jTy{\Gamma_1}{s_1}{A}$ and $j_2 = \jTy{\Gamma_2}{s_2}{A}$ where
$s_1$ and $s_2$ share a subterm $s$ independent of some variable $v \in \fv(j_1)
\setminus \fv(j_2)$, the required $j_{\mathsf{gen}}$ is a typing judgement
for $s$ in the projected context $\Gamma\!\downarrow_{\fv(s)}$.  Anti-unification
cannot produce this: it would need to invent a generalisation of $j_1$ and
$j_2$ that involves only $\fv(s)$, which requires knowing that $v \notin \fv(s)$
--- a \emph{dependency check}, not a structural operation.

Once we saw this, the implementation was direct: add a pre-pass that checks
$\indep{s}{v}$ (none of $v$'s indices appear free in $s$), and if so, project
the context.  The correctness of this projection is a roundtrip property
that we prove mechanically (Section~\ref{sec:speculation}).

\subsection{The architecture that follows}

Both gaps point to the same architectural pattern: extend the generalisation
step with a new layer that runs \emph{after} anti-unification fails:

\draw[arrow] (au)   -- node[above,font=\scriptsize]{None} (spec);

\draw[arrow] (llm)  -- node[above,font=\scriptsize]{None} (fail);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

The CIU theorem is indifferent to which layer produced the generalisation:
only the trace check matters.  This modularity is not an accident --- it is
a consequence of the sequent formulation, where soundness is established
at the level of the proof \emph{graph}, not the construction \emph{procedure}.
