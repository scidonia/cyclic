\subsection{Cyclic proof for reverse-append fusion}
\label{sec:reverse-append-proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:reverse-append-proof} shows the cyclic proof.
The root goal (bottom) is
$\mathsf{reverse}\;(\mathsf{append}\;\mathit{xs}\;\mathit{ys}) : \mathsf{List}$.
Unfolding $\mathsf{reverse}$ (rule \textsc{fix}) exposes
$\mathsf{revAcc}\;(\mathsf{append}\;\mathit{xs}\;\mathit{ys})\;\mathsf{nil}$.
The LLM-proposed lemma rewrites this to
$\mathsf{revAcc}\;\mathit{ys}\;(\mathsf{reverse}\;\mathit{xs})$ (rule
\textsc{lemma}).  Splitting on $\mathit{ys}$ ($\star$, progress event)
yields a nil branch that closes via cyclic backlink
($\mathit{ys}\mapsto\mathsf{nil}$), and a cons branch that drives further
through structural induction on $\mathit{ys}$.

The nil branch $\mathsf{reverse}\;\mathit{xs}$ is an instance of the root
goal $\mathsf{reverse}\;(\mathsf{append}\;\mathit{xs}\;\mathit{ys})$ under
the substitution $\mathit{ys}\mapsto\mathsf{nil}$, since
$\mathsf{reverse}\;\mathit{xs} = \mathsf{reverse}\;(\mathsf{append}\;\mathit{xs}\;\mathsf{nil})$.
The \textsc{back} rule closes the proof at this vertex.
Without the \textsc{lemma} rule, the step from
$\mathsf{revAcc}\;(\mathsf{append}\;\mathit{xs}\;\mathit{ys})\;\mathsf{nil}$
to $\mathsf{revAcc}\;\mathit{ys}\;(\mathsf{reverse}\;\mathit{xs})$ would
fail --- the two terms do not anti-unify.  The LLM proposes the lemma as a
cut; the sub-prover proves it by induction on $\mathit{xs}$; the main
proof uses it as a rewrite.
