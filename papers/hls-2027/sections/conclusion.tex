\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that a mechanised sequent-calculus foundation for
supercompilation is more than a correctness guarantee --- it is a
\emph{design instrument}.  By making precise the correspondence between SC
operations and sequent rules, we identified \emph{three} gaps in the standard theory:
generalisation is cut introduction (suggesting an oracle architecture),
speculation is variable projection (suggesting a dependency analysis),
and strengthened induction is the $\omega$-rule (suggesting LLM lemma
proposals with sub-SC validation).

All three extensions are implemented, mechanised, and provably correct:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The LLM oracle is structurally untrusted: any function
  \texttt{config -> list (config * nat) -> option gen\_result}
  preserves the CIU theorem.
\item The speculation roundtrip theorem (\texttt{canon\_subterm\_roundtrip})
  is proved with 0 Admitted in 16 lemmas.
\item The $\omega$-rule pipeline (LLM lemma proposal $\to$ sub-SC validation
  $\to$ lemma-driven SC retry) is fully mechanised and demonstrated on
  $\mathsf{reverse}\ (\mathsf{append}\ xs\ ys) = \mathsf{append}\
  (\mathsf{reverse}\ ys)\ (\mathsf{reverse}\ xs)$, a property the standard SC
  cannot fuse alone.
\item Together, the four-layer loop proves 76 theorems across 8 files,
  including all List and Maybe monad laws, insertion sort correctness,
  reverse-append distribution, and vector index normalisation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The central lesson.}
Mechanisation forced us to ask what generalisation \emph{is} at the level of
proof theory.  The answer --- cut introduction --- led to the oracle
architecture, the variable projection insight, and the $\omega$-rule
connection.  None of these were available from the operational account of
SC alone.  The sequent calculus was a design tool, not just a verification
tool.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The $\omega$-rule extension (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) is the immediate next
step: lemma environments in the driving step, validated by sub-SC runs, with
LLM-proposed auxiliary lemma statements.  This would bring $\mathsf{sorted}\ (\sort\ l) = \mathsf{true}$,
commutativity of plus, and the reversibility of permutations within reach.

Beyond HLS, the sequent perspective suggests studying SC as a decision
procedure for fragments of CIC: if the SC terminates and produces a residual
equal to a normal form, it has decided the term's observational class.  Making
this precise is a direction we plan to pursue.
