\begin{abstract}
A mechanised sequent-calculus foundation for supercompilation reveals three
precise gaps in the standard theory and points directly to their solutions.

Standard supercompilation corresponds to focused cyclic sequent proof search:
driving is cut elimination, generalisation is cut introduction.  Making this
correspondence precise in Rocq identified: (i)~generalisation is free cut
introduction --- suggesting an untrusted oracle validated by the kernel;
(ii)~strengthened induction is the $\omega$-rule --- suggesting LLM-proposed
auxiliary lemmas proved by a sub-supercompiler.

We implement both: an LLM oracle (DeepSeek V4 Pro) whose proposals are validated
by the existing trace-condition check so soundness is unconditional; and a
lemma environment where the LLM proposes cuts, a sub-SC proves them, and the
main SC uses them as rewrite rules.  We also prove that a third candidate
extension --- speculation as a generalisation step --- is redundant or unsound
in canonical configurations, a characterisation of the boundary between
anti-unification and projection.

The system, extracted to OCaml with a Python LLM bridge, proves 76 theorems
automatically (\texttt{vm\_compute}) with no human induction tactics, including
all List and Maybe monad laws, insertion sort correctness, a compiler
soundness theorem, gradual cast safety, and dependent-type branch elimination
that standard supercompilation cannot achieve.
\end{abstract}
