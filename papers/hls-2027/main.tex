\begin{abstract}
We present a mechanised account of \emph{higher-level supercompilation} (HLS)
in which the sequent calculus perspective --- developed to understand the
foundations of standard supercompilation --- directly suggested two productive
extensions that would not have been apparent otherwise.

Standard supercompilation corresponds to focused cyclic sequent proof search:
driving is cut elimination, generalisation is cut introduction. Making this
correspondence precise in Rocq revealed two gaps in the standard theory:
(i)~the generalisation step corresponds to free cut introduction, which in
proof theory is \emph{lemma synthesis}; (ii)~the termination argument for
loop-invariant subterms corresponds to a form of \emph{speculation} (Eisner-Blatz),
which requires a \emph{variable dependency analysis} absent from standard SC.

We implement both extensions: an LLM oracle for cut introduction, validated by the
existing proof kernel so soundness is unconditional; and a speculation
generalisation pass backed by a mechanised roundtrip theorem proving its
correctness.  The combined system, extracted to OCaml with a Python LLM bridge,
proves a striking collection of results entirely automatically by \vmcompute:
all three List monad laws, all three Maybe monad laws, the functor laws, plus
correctness properties of insertion sort (\texttt{sum~(sort~l)~=~sum~l},
\texttt{member~x~(insert~x~l)~=~true}) --- without any human-provided
induction tactics or lemma hints.
\end{abstract}
