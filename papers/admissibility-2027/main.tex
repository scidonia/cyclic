\begin{abstract}
Cyclic proofs close goals by backlinking to previously-seen configurations,
discovering induction principles post-hoc rather than prescribing them upfront.
We prove that backlinks are \emph{admissible} in the Calculus of Inductive
Constructions (CIC): every valid cyclic proof can be read back to a standard CIC
term that uses only the native fixpoint binder (\texttt{tFix}) for recursion
and is observationally indistinguishable from the root goal.  The proof is
a direct corollary of a contextual-equivalence theorem mechanised in Rocq with
0 axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cyclic proof theory~\cite{brotherston-2006-cyclic,brotherston-simpson-2011-sequent}
extends standard proof systems with backlink rules that allow a premise to
reference a previous goal, forming a directed cycle.  The cycle is sound if
it satisfies a global \emph{trace condition}: every cycle must contain a
\emph{progress event}---a case-split on a structurally smaller term.
Brotherston and Simpson prove that for first-order logic with inductive
definitions, backlinks are \emph{admissible}: every cyclic proof corresponds
to a standard proof with an explicit induction rule.

This paper extends their result to the Calculus of Inductive Constructions
(CIC)~\cite{coquand-huet-1988-coc,paulin-mohring-1993-inductive}, the type
theory underlying Rocq.  We prove that every rooted, ranked cyclic proof
can be residualised to a standard CIC term using only \texttt{tFix} for
recursion, and that the residual is observationally equivalent to the
root goal.

\section{Term calculus and reduction}

Terms are given by the grammar:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
t,u,A,B &::=& x \mid \Sort{i} \mid \Pi x{:}A.\,B \mid \lambda x{:}A.\,t \mid
            t\;u \mid \fix\, t \mid \Ind{I}{\vec{t}} \mid
            \Roll{I}{c}{\vec{t}} \mid \Case{I}{t}{\kappa}{\vec{b}}
\end{array}
\]
where $x$ ranges over de Bruijn indices, $i$ over universe levels,
$I$ identifies an inductive family, $c$ a constructor index,
$\kappa$ a dependent motive, and $\vec{t},\vec{b}$ are lists of terms.
Typing follows the standard CIC rules~\cite{coquand-huet-1988-coc,
paulin-mohring-1993-inductive}.

\paragraph{Operational semantics.}
We use a call-by-name (CBN) reduction relation $t \to t'$ defined by:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\beta$-reduction:}
  $(\lambda x{:}A.\,t)\;u \to t[u/x]$.

\item \textbf{Fixpoint unfolding:}
  $\fix\,t \to t[\fix\,t/0]$.  (The binder 0 in $t$ refers to the
  recursive self-reference.)

\item \textbf{$\iota$-reduction (case-of-constructor):}
  $\Case{I}{(\Roll{I}{c}{\vec{a}})}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} \to
    \mathsf{apps}\;(\vec{b}[c])\;\vec{a}$
  where $\vec{b}[c]$ selects the $c$-th branch.

\item \textbf{Congruence:} Reduction applies under applications and
  case scrutinees: if $t \to t'$ then $t\;u \to t'\;u$ and
  $\Case{I}{t}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} \to \Case{I}{t'}{\kappa}{\vec{b}}$.
\end{itemize}

A term $t$ \emph{terminates to} a value $v$, written $t \Downarrow v$,
if $t \to^* v$ and $v$ is in weak-head normal form (an abstraction,
a constructor, or a neutral term).

\section{Cyclic sequent calculus}

Configurations are typing judgements $\jTy{\Gamma}{t}{A}$.
A \emph{configuration graph} $b$ is a finite map from vertices
$v \in \{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ to configurations $\ell(b,v)$ and successor
lists $\sigma(b,v)$.  A graph is a \emph{rooted pre-proof} with root
$v_0$ if every vertex satisfies a local sequent rule drawn from the
following:

\paragraph{Asynchronous rules (deterministic).} Applied eagerly.
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Gamma \vdash t[u/x] : A}
  \infer1[\beta]{\Gamma \vdash (\lambda x{:}A.\,t)\;u : A}
\end{prooftree}
\qquad
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Gamma \vdash t[\fix\,t/0] : A}
  \infer1[\textsc{fix}]{\Gamma \vdash \fix\,t : A}
\end{prooftree}
\qquad
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{apps}\;(\vec{b}[c])\;\vec{a} : A}
  \infer1[\iota]{\Gamma \vdash \Case{I}{(\Roll{I}{c}{\vec{a}})}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} : A}
\end{prooftree}

\paragraph{Synchronous rule (choice point).}
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Gamma,\, \mathit{constraints} \vdash t_0 : A}
  \hypo{\Gamma,\, \mathit{constraints} \vdash t_1 : A}
  \infer2[\textsc{split}]{\Gamma \vdash \Case{I}{x}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} : A}
\end{prooftree}
where $x$ is a neutral variable and each premise corresponds to one
constructor of $I$, with fresh variables for its arguments.

\paragraph{Cyclic backlink (closure).}
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Delta \vdash \sigma :_s \Gamma}
  \infer1[\textsc{back}]{\jTy{\Delta}{t}{A}}
\end{prooftree}
where $t$ is an instance of a previously-seen configuration
$\jTy{\Gamma}{t_0}{A}$ under the explicit substitution $\sigma$.

\paragraph{Global progress condition.}
A rooted pre-proof is a \emph{rooted cyclic proof} if every directed
cycle contains at least one \textsc{split} edge.  This is witnessed by
a budget-trace ranking: vertices are lifted to pairs $(v,k)$ where
$k \in [0,|V|]$.  \textsc{split} edges consume budget ($k \mapsto k-1$);
all other edges preserve it.  The lifted graph must be acyclic.
We write $\mathcal{C}(b,v_0)$ for this property.

\section{Residualisation}
\label{sec:residualise}

The backlink rule appears only in the proof graph.  A \emph{residualisation}
function $\mathcal{R}(b,v)$ reads back the graph into a CIC term by
traversing the successors of $v$ and replacing backlinks with
\texttt{tFix} binders:

\begin{itemize}
\item A vertex with no successors returns its own term.
\item A vertex with one successor applies the driving step (unfold,
  $\beta$-reduce, apply constructor) to the successor's residual.
\item A vertex that is the target of a backlink becomes a \texttt{tFix}
  whose body is the residual of its successors, with recursive calls
  referencing the \texttt{tFix} binder.
\end{itemize}

The result is a well-typed CIC term using only standard constructors
(\texttt{tLam}, \texttt{tApp}, \texttt{tFix}, \texttt{tCase},
\texttt{tRoll}).  No backlink constructs remain.

\section{CIU equivalence}

The soundness guarantee is \emph{CIU equivalence} (contextual
equivalence under all closing substitutions):
\[
  t \ciu u \;\equiv\; \forall \sigma, v.\; (t[\sigma] \Downarrow v \iff
  u[\sigma] \Downarrow v)
\]

The mechanised CIU theorem (companion paper, under review) establishes that residualisation
preserves equivalence:

\begin{theorem}[CIU soundness]
\label{thm:ciu}
If $b$ is a rooted, ranked cyclic proof with root $v$ and
$\ell(b,v) = \jTy{\Gamma}{t}{A}$, then
\[
  t \ciu \mathcal{R}(b,v).
\]
\end{theorem}

\noindent The proof (mechanised in Rocq, 0 axioms) is by fuel induction
on the residualiser.  Congruence lemmas lift CIU through all nine term
constructors.  The trace condition excludes non-terminating self-loops.

\section{Admissibility}

\begin{theorem}[Backlink admissibility]
\label{thm:admissible}
For every configuration graph $b$ and vertex $v$,
\[
  \mathcal{C}(b,v)
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \exists\, t_{\mathsf{std}},\;
  t \ciu t_{\mathsf{std}}
  \;\land\;
  t_{\mathsf{std}} = \mathcal{R}(b,v)
\]
where $\ell(b,v) = \jTy{\Gamma}{t}{A}$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent In words: every rooted cyclic proof can be read back to a
standard CIC term (using only \texttt{tFix} for recursion) that is
observationally indistinguishable from the root goal.

\begin{proof}
Take $t_{\mathsf{std}} = \mathcal{R}(b,v)$.  By construction,
$t_{\mathsf{std}}$ uses only standard CIC constructors
(Section~\ref{sec:residualise}).  The equality $t \ciu t_{\mathsf{std}}$
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:ciu}.  $\square$
\end{proof}

\noindent The mechanised proof (\texttt{BacklinkAdmissible.v}) is a
four-line corollary of the CIU theorem.

\section{Discussion}

The admissibility transformation is the same residualisation that the
proof graph already provides.  The CIU theorem proves equivalence; the
residualiser eliminates backlinks; the result follows trivially.

Compared to Brotherston and Simpson's result for first-order logic, our
proof is simpler because (a)~the graph is finite, and (b)~residualisation
is a syntactic transformation that explicitly replaces backlinks with
\texttt{tFix}, rather than requiring a semantic argument about proof
unfoldings.
