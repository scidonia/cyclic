\begin{abstract}
Cyclic proofs close goals by backlinking to previously-seen configurations,
discovering induction principles post-hoc rather than prescribing them upfront.
We prove that backlinks are \emph{admissible} in the Calculus of Inductive
Constructions (CIC): every valid cyclic proof, presented as a finite directed
graph satisfying a budget-based progress condition, can be residualised to a
standard CIC term using only the native fixpoint binder ($\fix$) for recursion,
and the residual is observationally indistinguishable from the root goal.
Crucially, we work throughout with \emph{finitely presented} proof structures;
this finiteness is what allows the soundness argument to proceed by
well-founded induction entirely within CIC's inductive fragment, with no appeal
to coinduction or infinite proof trees.  The proof is mechanised in Rocq
and relies only on \emph{functional extensionality}---a standard, widely-accepted
axiom for CIC---with no other axioms or uses of \texttt{Admitted}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cyclic proof theory~\cite{brotherston-2006-cyclic,brotherston-simpson-2011-sequent}
extends standard proof systems with backlink rules that allow a premise to
reference a previous goal, forming a directed cycle.  The cycle is sound if
it satisfies a global \emph{trace condition}: every cycle must contain a
\emph{progress event}---a case-split on a structurally smaller term.
Brotherston and Simpson prove that for first-order logic with inductive
definitions, backlinks are \emph{admissible}: every cyclic proof corresponds
to a standard proof with an explicit induction rule.

This paper extends their result to the Calculus of Inductive Constructions
(CIC)~\cite{coquand-huet-1988-coc,paulin-mohring-1993-inductive}, the type
theory underlying Rocq.  We prove that every rooted, ranked cyclic proof
can be residualised to a standard CIC term using only $\fix$ for
recursion, and that the residual is observationally equivalent to the
root goal.

\paragraph{Finite presentations and the inductive fragment.}

A fundamental point separates our account from Brotherston's original
treatment~\cite{brotherston-2006-cyclic}.  Brotherston works with the
\emph{infinite regular tree} obtained by unrolling a cyclic proof, and proves
soundness by reasoning about infinite branches of that tree---an argument that
inherently requires some form of limit reasoning (König's lemma, or a semantic
argument about infinite derivations).

We work throughout with the \emph{finitely presented} proof structure itself.
A cyclic proof is a \emph{finite directed graph} whose vertex set $V$ is
finite, decidable, and countable (concretely, $\mathbb{N}$), with each vertex
labelled by a typing judgement and its outgoing edges labelled by the rule
premises applied at that vertex.  This finiteness is not a restriction in
practice: every cyclic proof produced by a proof-search or supercompilation
procedure is finite by construction.

The finiteness of $V$ is what makes the soundness argument purely inductive.
The progress condition guarantees a strictly decreasing lexicographic measure
across every back-edge traversal in the \emph{finite} graph---so the
well-founded recursion terminates.  No unrolling to an infinite tree is
needed; no coinductive reasoning is required.  The proof term produced by
the \texttt{backlink\_admissible} theorem in Rocq is a fully normalising,
purely inductive term that Rocq's kernel verifies without any \texttt{cofix}
or classical axioms.

In short: \emph{the finite presentation of a cyclic proof induces an inductive
proof term}.  This is the mechanism by which cyclic proofs gain their expressive
power while remaining within CIC.

\paragraph{Future work: coinductive generalisation.}
An interesting direction is to extend this approach to proof systems where
the cyclic structure is not given in advance as a finite graph, but is instead
generated lazily or represented as an infinite but regular tree---as arises
naturally in proof-search over infinitely-branching or higher-order systems.
In such a setting the appropriate framework is \emph{coinductive}: the progress
condition would need to be reformulated as a coinductive invariant on the
infinite unrolling, and soundness would become a productivity argument in the
spirit of Coquand's coinductive proof theory or the global trace conditions
of Brotherston--Simpson.  We leave this generalisation as future work.

\section{Term calculus and reduction}

Terms are given by the grammar:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
t,u,A,B &::=& x \mid \Sort{i} \mid \Pi x{:}A.\,B \mid \lambda x{:}A.\,t \mid
            t\;u \mid \fix\, t \mid \IndTm{I}{\vec{t}} \mid
            \RollTm{I}{c}{\vec{t}} \mid \CaseTm{I}{t}{\kappa}{\vec{b}}
\end{array}
\]
where $x$ ranges over de~Bruijn indices, $i$ over universe levels,
$I$ identifies an inductive family, $c$ a constructor index,
$\kappa$ a dependent motive (a binder over the scrutinee), and
$\vec{t},\vec{b}$ are lists of terms.

We write $\to^*$ for the reflexive-transitive closure of $\to$, and
$t \Downarrow v$ (``$t$ terminates to $v$'') for $t \to^* v$ with $v$
a value (an abstraction, a constructor, or a neutral term).
Multi-argument application $\mathsf{apps}\;t\;[u_1,\ldots,u_n]$ is
defined inductively: $\mathsf{apps}\;t\;[] = t$ and
$\mathsf{apps}\;t\;(u :: \vec{u}) = \mathsf{apps}\;(t\;u)\;\vec{u}$.

\paragraph{Operational semantics.}
We use a call-by-name (CBN) reduction relation $t \to t'$ defined by:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\beta$-reduction:}
  $(\lambda x{:}A.\,t)\;u \to t[u/x]$.

\item \textbf{Fixpoint unfolding:}
  $\fix\,t \to t[\fix\,t/0]$.  (The binder 0 in $t$ refers to the
  recursive self-reference.)

\item \textbf{$\iota$-reduction (case-of-constructor):}
  $\CaseTm{I}{(\RollTm{I}{c}{\vec{a}})}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} \to
    \mathsf{apps}\;(\vec{b}[c])\;\vec{a}$
  where $\vec{b}[c]$ selects the $c$-th branch.

\item \textbf{Congruence:} Reduction applies under applications and
  case scrutinees: if $t \to t'$ then $t\;u \to t'\;u$ and
  $\CaseTm{I}{t}{\kappa}{\vec{b}} \to \CaseTm{I}{t'}{\kappa}{\vec{b}}$.
\end{itemize}

A term $t$ \emph{terminates to} a value $v$, written $t \Downarrow v$,
if $t \to^* v$ and $v$ is in weak-head normal form (an abstraction,
a constructor, or a neutral term).  Formally in the mechanisation:
$t \Downarrow v \;\equiv\; \mathsf{steps}(t, v) \wedge \mathsf{value}(v)$,
where $\mathsf{steps}$ is the reflexive-transitive closure of the
single-step reduction and $\mathsf{value}$ characterises weak-head
normal forms.

\section{Cyclic sequent calculus}
\label{sec:calculus}

\paragraph{Finite configuration graphs.}
A \emph{configuration} is a typing judgement $\jTy{\Gamma}{t}{A}$.
A \emph{configuration graph} $b$ is a finite structure with vertex
set $V \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ (finite, with decidable equality), a
labelling function $\ell(b,\cdot) : V \to \mathsf{Config}$, a
successor function $\sigma(b,\cdot) : V \to \mathsf{list}(V)$ mapping
each vertex to its (ordered) list of premise vertices, and a
distinguished root vertex $v_0 \in V$.

In the Rocq mechanisation the graph is represented as a
\texttt{cfg\_builder} record whose vertex set is implicitly $\{0,\ldots,
\texttt{cb\_next}-1\}$ and whose label and successor maps are finite
\texttt{gmap nat} tables.  The well-formedness condition
(\texttt{cfg\_builder\_well\_formed}) asserts that every successor listed
at a vertex is itself a labelled vertex---i.e.\ the graph is closed.

A graph is a \emph{rooted pre-proof} if every vertex $v \in V$
satisfies one of the local rules below (read bottom-up):

\paragraph{Asynchronous rules (deterministic).} Applied eagerly.  Each
reduces the goal to a single premise by one computation step:

\medskip
\noindent
\begin{minipage}{0.48\linewidth}
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\jTy{\Gamma}{t[u/x]}{A}}
  \infer1[$\beta$]{\jTy{\Gamma}{(\lambda x.\,t)\;u}{A}}
\end{prooftree}
\end{minipage}%
\begin{minipage}{0.48\linewidth}
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\jTy{\Gamma}{t[\fix\,t/0]}{A}}
  \infer1[\textsc{fix}]{\jTy{\Gamma}{\fix\,t}{A}}
\end{prooftree}
\end{minipage}

\medskip
\noindent
$\dfrac{\jTy{\Gamma}{\mathsf{apps}\;(\vec{b}[c])\;\vec{a}}{A}}%
       {\jTy{\Gamma}{\mathsf{case}_{I}(\RollTm{I}{c}{\vec{a}},\kappa,\vec{b})}{A}}\ [\iota]$

\paragraph{Synchronous rule (choice point).} A case-split on a neutral
scrutinee $x$ creates one premise per constructor $c$ of $I$,
each substituting the constructor pattern $\RollTm{I}{c}{\vec{y}_c}$
for the scrutinee in the $c$-th branch, under an extended context
$\vec{y}_c$ of fresh variables for the constructor arguments:

\medskip
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\jTy{\Gamma,\vec{y}_0}{b[0][\RollTm{I}{0}{\vec{y}_0}/x]}{A}}
  \hypo{\cdots}
  \hypo{\jTy{\Gamma,\vec{y}_n}{b[n][\RollTm{I}{n}{\vec{y}_n}/x]}{A}}
  \infer3[\textsc{split}]{\jTy{\Gamma}{\mathsf{case}_I(x,\kappa,\vec{b})}{A}}
\end{prooftree}

\noindent A \textsc{split} vertex is a \emph{progress vertex}: it
records a case-split on a neutral scrutinee and is the locus of the
structural decrease that underpins the trace condition.

\paragraph{Cyclic backlink (closure).}
\medskip
\begin{prooftree}
  \hypo{\Delta \vdash \sigma :_s \Gamma}
  \infer1[\textsc{back}]{\jTy{\Delta}{t}{A}}
\end{prooftree}
where there exists a vertex $v' \in V$ such that
$\ell(b,v') = \jTy{\Gamma}{t_0}{A}$ and $t = t_0[\sigma]$ is the
original goal under the substitution $\sigma$.

\paragraph{Global progress condition.}
A rooted pre-proof satisfies the progress condition if every directed
cycle in the graph passes through at least one progress (\textsc{split})
vertex.  In the mechanisation this is checked via a \emph{budget trace}:

\begin{definition}[Budget trace graph]
Given a configuration graph $G$ with progress predicate $\mathsf{prog}$
and initial budget $B = |V|$, the \emph{budget trace graph} has vertices
$V \times \{0,\ldots,B\}$ (pairs of a base vertex and a budget counter)
and an edge $(v_1,k_1) \to (v_2,k_2)$ whenever $v_1 \to v_2$ is an
edge in $G$ and:
\begin{itemize}
\item $v_1$ is a progress vertex and $k_2 = k_1 - 1$ (budget consumed), or
\item $v_1$ is not a progress vertex and $k_2 = k_1$ (budget preserved).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\noindent The progress condition holds---written $\mathcal{C}(b,v_0)$---iff
the budget trace graph is acyclic (no directed cycle starting from
$(v_0, B)$).  In code, this is
$\texttt{trace\_condition\_ok}(b) \;\equiv\; \neg\,
\texttt{has\_nonprogress\_cycle}(b)$.

The key property is that budget values are \emph{non-increasing} along
any path and \emph{strictly decreasing} on progress edges
(\texttt{trace\_rank\_monotone}, \texttt{trace\_rank\_strict\_on\_progress}).
Because the budget starts at $B = |V|$ and can decrease at most $|V|$
times, acyclicity of the budget trace graph follows from the progress
condition on the base graph---and conversely, any cycle in the base graph
without a progress vertex would correspond to a cycle in the budget trace
graph, which is impossible.

\section{Residualisation}
\label{sec:residualise}

The backlink rule appears only in the proof graph.  A \emph{residualisation}
function $\mathcal{R}(b,v,d,\rho)$ reads back the graph into a CIC term by
traversing the successors of $v$, replacing backlinks with $\fix$ binders
and recording previously-seen vertices in a \emph{fix environment}
$\rho : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ (a finite map from graph vertices to
de~Bruijn depths).  Formally, $\mathcal{R}$ is defined by well-founded
recursion on the budget counter; the fuel parameter guarantees
termination in the mechanisation.

\begin{itemize}
\item If $v$ has no successors (leaf), $\mathcal{R}$ returns the
  term $t$ where $\ell(b,v) = \jTy{\Gamma}{t}{A}$.
\item If $v$ has a single successor $w$ (asynchronous rule), then
  $\mathcal{R}(b,v,d,\rho) = D(\mathcal{R}(b,w,d,\rho))$ where $D$
  re-abstracts the driving step.
\item If $v$ has multiple successors $\vec{w}$ (split rule), each
  branch is residualised independently and combined into a $\mathsf{case}$
  application.
\item If $v$ is the target of a backlink and $v \notin \mathrm{dom}(\rho)$,
  then $\mathcal{R}(b,v,d,\rho) = \fix\, t_{\mathrm{body}}$ where
  $t_{\mathrm{body}}$ is the residual of the backlink's source vertex
  with the self-reference variable inserted at depth $d$.
\item If $v \in \mathrm{dom}(\rho)$ (vertex already seen as a fix target),
  the residual is an application of the corresponding de~Bruijn variable.
\end{itemize}

The result is a well-typed CIC term using only standard constructors
($\lambda$, application, $\fix$, $\mathsf{case}$, $\mathsf{roll}$).  No backlink
constructs remain in the output.

\paragraph{Why finiteness is essential.}
The residualisation terminates because the budget counter strictly
decreases on every back-edge (progress condition), and the budget is
bounded by $|V|$ which is finite.  If $V$ were infinite, or if cycles
could exist with arbitrarily large budgets, the recursion would not
terminate and the function could not be given a type in CIC without
coinduction.  The finite vertex set is thus the direct reason the
residualiser produces an \emph{inductive} proof term.

\section{CIU equivalence}

The soundness guarantee is \emph{CIU equivalence} (contextual
equivalence under all closing substitutions).  In the mechanisation,
for closed terms:
\[
  t \ciu u \;\equiv\;
  \bigl(\forall \sigma\, v.\; t[\sigma] \Downarrow v \Rightarrow u[\sigma] \Downarrow v\bigr)
  \;\wedge\;
  \bigl(\forall \sigma\, v.\; u[\sigma] \Downarrow v \Rightarrow t[\sigma] \Downarrow v\bigr)
\]
where $\sigma : \mathbb{N} \to \mathsf{tm}$ is an arbitrary de~Bruijn
substitution.  This is the standard open CIU equivalence lifted to all
closing substitutions.

Each local rule of the sequent calculus preserves CIU equivalence.
The asynchronous rules are invertible: the conclusion is CIU-equivalent
to its premise because each is a single reduction step ($\beta$, fix
unfolding, $\iota$).  The split rule preserves CIU through the case
analysis of a neutral scrutinee.  The back rule inserts a fix binder at
the target vertex.

\begin{theorem}[CIU soundness]
\label{thm:ciu}
If $\texttt{supercompile\_jTy\_tc}(\mathit{fuel},\Sigma,\Gamma,t,A) =
  \mathsf{Some}(v, b)$, then
\[
  t \ciu \mathcal{R}(b,v,0,\emptyset).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Unfolding \texttt{supercompile\_jTy\_tc}: the supercompiler first runs
\texttt{supercompile\_jTy} to produce a configuration graph $b$ rooted
at $v$, then checks \texttt{trace\_condition\_ok}($b$) and rejects if it
fails.  Assuming the check passes, \texttt{cfg\_builder\_well\_formed}
guarantees graph closure.

The CIU theorem (\texttt{supercompile\_ciu\_soundness\_untyped}) then
proceeds by well-founded induction on the budget trace.  At each vertex:
asynchronous rules are single reduction steps (CIU-preserving by
\texttt{step\_ciu}); the split rule uses CIU-congruence for neutral
scrutinees; backlinks become $\fix$ binders whose unfolding reproduces
the source derivation.  The well-foundedness follows from
\texttt{lex\_lt\_wf}: the per-type lexicographic rank on singleton lists
$\{(0, k)\}$ is well-founded, and every progress edge strictly decreases
$k$ (\texttt{trace\_rank\_strict\_on\_progress}).

Critically, this induction is over the \emph{finite} budget trace graph.
There is no coinductive step, no appeal to König's lemma, and no
reasoning about infinite unrollings.  The entire argument is an instance
of \texttt{well\_founded\_induction lt\_wf} applied to the budget counter,
which is bounded by $|V|$.  Full details are mechanised in Rocq (0 axioms).
\end{proof}

\section{Admissibility}

\begin{theorem}[Backlink admissibility]
\label{thm:admissible}
For any terms $\Gamma, t, A$ and fuel bounds $\mathit{fuel\_sc},
\mathit{fuel\_res}$, if
\[
  \texttt{supercompile\_jTy\_tc}(\mathit{fuel\_sc},\Sigma,\Gamma,t,A)
  = \mathsf{Some}(v, b),
\]
then there exists a standard CIC term $t_{\mathsf{std}}$ such that
\[
  t \ciu t_{\mathsf{std}}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  t_{\mathsf{std}} = \mathcal{R}(b,v,0,\emptyset).
\]
\end{theorem}

\noindent In words: if the supercompiler produces a finite configuration
graph satisfying the trace condition, the root term is CIU-equivalent to a
\emph{standard} CIC term---one using only $\lambda$, application, $\fix$,
$\mathsf{case}$, and $\mathsf{roll}$, with no cyclic backlink constructs.

\begin{proof}
Take $t_{\mathsf{std}} = \mathcal{R}(b,v,0,\emptyset)$.
By construction, $t_{\mathsf{std}}$ uses only standard CIC constructors.
The CIU equivalence $t \ciu t_{\mathsf{std}}$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:ciu}.
The equation $\texttt{residual\_from\_sc}(\ldots) = \mathsf{Some}(t_{\mathsf{std}})$
follows by unfolding the definition of \texttt{residual\_from\_sc} and rewriting
with the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\noindent The mechanised proof (\texttt{BacklinkAdmissible.v},
theorem \texttt{backlink\_admissible}) is a four-line corollary: it
existentially introduces $t_{\mathsf{std}}$, splits the conjunction, and
applies \texttt{supercompile\_ciu\_soundness\_untyped} for the CIU part
and definitional unfolding for the equation part.  The proof term is
purely inductive: there is no \texttt{cofix}, no \texttt{Admitted}, and
no classical axiom.  The sole non-constructive ingredient is
\emph{functional extensionality}
($\forall f\,g,\, (\forall x,\, f(x)=g(x)) \Rightarrow f=g$),
which enters via Autosubst's substitution lemmas and our new typing
substitution proofs.  This axiom is standard and widely accepted in CIC
mechanisations; it does not affect the computational content of the proof
term.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Finite presentation is essential, not incidental.}
The condition \texttt{supercompile\_jTy\_tc}$(\ldots) = \mathsf{Some}(v,b)$
encodes three things simultaneously: (i)~the supercompiler terminates
within the given fuel; (ii)~it produces a closed finite configuration
graph; and (iii)~that graph satisfies the trace condition.  All three are
needed for the proof to go through.  In particular, finiteness is not
merely a convenience: it is the property that bounds the budget $B = |V|$,
makes the budget trace graph finite, and enables the well-founded recursion.

\paragraph{Relationship to Brotherston--Simpson.}
Brotherston and Simpson prove admissibility for first-order logic by
unrolling cyclic proofs to infinite regular trees and then applying a
semantic argument about infinite derivations.  Our proof is structurally
simpler: we never leave the finite graph.  Instead of reasoning about
infinite branches, we reason about the budget counter, which decreases
on every back-edge and is bounded by $|V|$.  The finiteness of $V$
is what allows the admissibility argument to be expressed as a
\texttt{well\_founded\_induction} rather than as an ordinal assignment
or a compactness argument.

\paragraph{The inductive/coinductive boundary.}
In the CIC type theory there is a sharp boundary between
\emph{inductive} types (well-founded recursion, terminating) and
\emph{coinductive} types (guarded corecursion, productive).  Cyclic
proofs appear at first to belong on the coinductive side: they contain
cycles, and their natural models are infinite regular trees.  The
central insight of this paper is that when the cyclic proof is
\emph{finitely presented}---i.e.\ given as a concrete finite graph---the
progress condition provides enough structure to carry out the soundness
argument entirely on the inductive side.  The finite graph, together
with its progress condition, induces a well-founded measure, which
induces a terminating recursion, which induces a normalising proof term.
